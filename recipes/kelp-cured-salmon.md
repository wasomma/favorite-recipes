# Kelp-Cured Salmon (Kobujime)

Costco salmon salted for an hour, rinsed, wrapped in rehydrated kelp, and left in the fridge for two days. It comes out firm, slightly translucent, and deeply savory, eaten raw in slices or on rice as sushi. The Korean name on the source page is gonbujume, from the Japanese kobujime.

- **Method:** No cook. Salt, then cure wrapped in kelp in the fridge.
- **Time:** 1 hour salting plus 2 to 3 days in the fridge
- **Serves:** 6
- **First made:** Not yet. Tasted at a Korean friend's house, October 2026. This is their recipe.

## Ingredients

**Salmon**

- 2.2 lb (1 kg) Costco raw salmon, trimmed, pin bones removed
- Coarse salt (sea salt or kosher), enough to coat every piece
- Dried kelp (Korean dasima or Japanese dashi kombu), enough sheets to wrap all the salmon

**Optional**

- A few sprigs of fresh dill

## Steps

1. **Prep.** Sanitize the cutting board, knife, and curing container with hot water. Check the salmon for bones and cut it into bite-size pieces.
2. **Salt.** Rinse the salmon, coat every piece with coarse salt, and let it rest for 1 hour. Water will bead on the surface and the flesh will firm up.
3. **Soak the kelp.** While the salmon salts, rinse the kelp well and soak it in water until it bends without cracking, about 10 to 20 minutes.
4. **Rinse.** Rinse the salt off the salmon thoroughly, drain well, and pat dry.
5. **Wrap.** Lay kelp in the bottom of a lidded container, arrange the salmon on it in a single layer, and cover with more kelp so no salmon shows. Wide sheets are easiest, but any wrap that covers the fish works.
6. **Cure.** Put the lid on and refrigerate for 2 to 3 days. After 2 days the salmon is matured. The source gave it a third day.
7. **Serve.** Unwrap, discard the kelp or save it for soup stock, and slice thinly. Eat as is, as sashimi, or on rice as sushi.

## Notes

- **Sake variation.** At the table the kelp was remembered as soaked in sake, not water. That is the traditional Japanese step: wipe the kelp with a damp cloth, keep the white powder on it (that is the umami), and brush it with sake until pliable. Swap this in for step 3 if you want more kelp flavor. Rinsing and water-soaking as the source does is milder.
- **Shorter cure.** Overnight gives a light cure with the fish still mostly tasting of salmon. Two days is the source's call. Past about 3 days it turns gummy and tastes mostly of kelp.
- **Safety.** The cure does not kill parasites. Farmed Atlantic salmon is widely eaten raw and carries very low parasite risk, but Costco does not label it sushi-grade. To be careful, freeze the fillet solid for a week first, then thaw in the fridge before salting.
- **Kelp.** Any Asian market sells dasima or dashi kombu in the dried seaweed aisle. Thick, wide pieces work best. Korean dasima and Japanese kombu are the same seaweed.
- **Serving ideas beyond the source.** Soy sauce and wasabi, or ponzu with grated ginger. Korean style: chojang (gochujang thinned with rice vinegar, a little sugar, and sesame oil) with perilla or lettuce wraps, raw garlic slices, and sliced green chili.
- Keeps about 2 days in the fridge after unwrapping.
