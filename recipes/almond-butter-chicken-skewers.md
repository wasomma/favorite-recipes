# Almond Butter Chicken Skewers

Chicken thigh strips marinated in a satay-style almond butter, soy, lime and ginger sauce, charred on the griddle and served with the same sauce for dipping.

- **Method:** Blackstone griddle (air fryer variant below)
- **Time:** at least 2 hours marinating, then about 15 minutes
- **Serves:** 6
- **First made:** Not yet (planned November 2026)

## Ingredients

**Sauce**

- 1/2 cup almond butter, stirred well
- 4 1/2 tbsp soy sauce, plus up to 1 tbsp more to taste
- 3 tbsp lime juice
- 1 1/2 tbsp minced ginger
- 1 1/2 tbsp minced garlic
- 1 1/2 tsp curry powder
- 6 tbsp chicken broth, plus more to thin
- 1/2 tsp crushed red pepper

**Chicken**

- 3 lb boneless, skinless chicken thighs, cut into 1 1/2-inch strips
- Avocado oil for the griddle
- Wooden skewers, soaked 30 minutes (optional)

**To serve**

- Cooked rice or romaine leaves for wraps
- Cherry tomatoes and sliced mini sweet peppers
- Sesame seeds

## Steps

1. **Make the sauce.** Whisk everything together. Taste it, and add up to 1 tbsp more soy if it tastes flat. Add broth a tablespoon at a time until it coats a spoon like heavy cream.
2. **Split the sauce.** Set half aside for dipping before it touches any raw chicken.
3. **Marinate.** Coat the chicken with the other half. Refrigerate at least 2 hours, or overnight.
4. **Prep.** Thread the strips onto skewers, or leave them flat. Wipe off excess marinade so the chicken chars instead of burning.
5. **Preheat.** Heat the Blackstone to medium, not high. Nut butters scorch fast. Oil it well.
6. **Cook.** Lay the chicken down and turn it every 3 to 4 minutes, 12 to 14 minutes total, until it reaches 175°F inside.
7. **Rescue if needed.** If the outside darkens before the inside is done, move it to a cooler zone and cover with a dome for the last few minutes.
8. **Dipping sauce.** Thin the reserved sauce with a splash of broth or warm water and a squeeze of lime.
9. **Serve.** Serve with rice or romaine wraps, tomatoes and peppers on the side, and a sprinkle of sesame seeds.

## Notes

- Natural almond butter separates. Stir the oil back in before measuring, or the sauce turns greasy.
- Almond butter is often unsalted, which is why the soy is adjustable.
- Peanut butter works in the same amount for a classic satay flavor. It is usually saltier, so skip the extra soy.
- Flat strips are easier to turn than skewers and char just as well.
- Go by the 175°F reading, not the clock.

## Air fryer variant

For a smaller batch (up to about 1 lb), air fry at 400°F for 12 to 16 minutes, flipping halfway. A full 3 lb needs three or four batches, so the griddle is faster.
